# manyworldz: two-week verdict, 2026-08-20

The campaign's real start was 2026-08-06 (the first 75 cron runs failed on a missing API key; the first real picks landed that evening). Today is two weeks later.

## The numbers

- Total picks logged: 6
- Settled picks: 0
- Wins: 0
- Losses: 0
- Average CLV: 0.0 cents, but this is not a real number yet. All 6 picks are still open. The 0.0 cents is just where the current mid price happens to sit against the entry price, not a closing-line result.
- Total spend: $3.59 of a $10.00 cap, across 1,680 API calls

## Did the cron keep running?

Yes. I checked the commit history from 2026-08-08 through 2026-08-20 and the cron committed a "ledger + dashboard update" roughly every 6 hours the entire time, four times a day, with no gap longer than a day. The automation did its job.

What the cron did not do is add picks. Of the 50 commits in that window, only one of them actually added a new row to the ledger (on 2026-08-18, a Jon Ossoff pick). For 11 straight days, from 2026-08-07 to 2026-08-18, the ledger sat at 5 picks while the cron ran on schedule and produced nothing new. So this is not an infrastructure failure. It is a pick-generation failure: the engine is running but is not finding or logging picks most of the time.

## The verdict

**Too early to judge.** Zero picks have settled. The project's own gates (GATES.md) call for at least 300 to 350 settled, graded picks before anyone judges whether the crowd beats the market. We are at 0. The honest answer right now is not "winning" or "losing," it is "no data yet." The campaign should simply keep running.

## The concentration problem

The known caveat from day one has not gone away. It got worse.

On day one, 4 of the first 5 picks were all for the same market, the 2028 Democratic presidential nominee, priced independently for four different candidates (Newsom, AOC, Mark Kelly, Kamala Harris). Their crowd probabilities summed to about 157 percent in a field where only one candidate can win. That is the known bug.

Two weeks later, the picture is the same, only more concentrated. 5 of the 6 total picks logged are for that same nominee market (the four from day one plus Jon Ossoff, added on 2026-08-18). Their crowd probabilities now sum to about 197 percent. The only pick outside that market is a single "Citrini scenario" market from day one. The crowd has not spread out to new markets. It has piled further into the same mutually exclusive longshot field.

Whether this concentration is helping or hurting CLV cannot be answered yet, because nothing has settled. The interim numbers (comparing entry price to the current mid, not a closing price) are a wash: 3 of the 6 picks show positive movement and 3 show negative, and they cancel out to exactly 0.0 cents. There is no visible pattern yet, good or bad. This question needs settled data to answer honestly, and there is none.

One more thing worth flagging: the nominee market and the Citrini scenario market are both long-dated. They may not close for a long time. If the engine keeps logging picks only in markets like these, the ledger may struggle to accumulate settled picks at all, no matter how long the cron keeps running.

## Next step

Redirect the engine toward markets that actually close soon instead of piling more picks into the same long-dated 2028 nominee field, so the ledger can start accumulating settled picks toward the 300-plus the project's own gates require.
